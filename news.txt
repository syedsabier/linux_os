Women’s organisations marched to the Delhi Police headquarters on Saturday, accusing police personnel of sexually harassing protesters during demonstrations against electoral roll revisions and demanding action against the officers allegedly responsible.

Communist Party of India (CPI) leader Annie Raja escalated the allegations, accusing the Centre of using sexual violence to suppress dissent and demanding the resignation of Union Home Minister Amit Shah.
The demonstration brought together several women’s organisations, including the All India Democratic Women’s Association (AIDWA), National Federation of Indian Women (NFIW) and All India Progressive Women’s Association (AIPWA).

The protesters alleged that women journalists and activists had faced inappropriate physical contact, intimidation and detention during demonstrations against the Election Commission of India (ECI).

The allegations emerged amid continuing protests against the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, with opposition groups demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over alleged irregularities and voter exclusions.

Annie Raja targets Amit Shah over police action
Raja alleged that the treatment of women protesters was intended to discourage their participation in public demonstrations.

“To silence us, to disappear the women of this country who are participating in the protest and occupying the public space, the fascist Amit Shah government or Modi government are using sexual assault as a weapon irrespective of age,” she told ANI.

Another demonstrator accused the police of attempting to intimidate people protesting against the Election Commission.

“The brutality shown by the Delhi Police should stop. No one can stop us from protesting against the ECI; this is our democratic right. We cannot tolerate the shamelessness shown by the police towards the women protesters,” the activist said.

The demonstrators demanded an end to the alleged harassment and called for legal action against personnel found responsible.

The latest demonstration followed allegations of sexual harassment made by three women journalists covering an earlier protest near Jantar Mantar. Delhi Police had assigned the investigation into those allegations to its Crime Branch.

Fresh FIRs after Connaught Place protest
The protest at the police headquarters came a day after demonstrations in Connaught Place, where protesters assembled for the ‘Lehra Do Tiranga’ march against the Election Commission.

Delhi Police registered multiple first information reports (FIRs) over alleged violations of prohibitory orders during Friday’s demonstrations.

Police officials said restrictions under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) were in force and that repeated announcements had been made asking people not to assemble.

The police maintained that action was taken over violations of the restrictions.

Among those detained on Friday were Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) founder Abhijeet Dipke and All India Students’ Association (AISA) president Neha Bora.

Bora was released early Saturday after being held overnight, while police initiated preventive proceedings against Dipke.

The detentions drew criticism from opposition parties and student organisations, which questioned the restrictions imposed on protesters.

The women’s organisations, meanwhile, maintained that their demonstrations would continue until action was taken over the alleged misconduct and the authorities addressed their demands.
